Dr. Gold — the overtesting question, both directions.

You pressed on the overtesting trap — ordering the secondary, more-invasive test when the data already answers the question — and on early closure, dropping a real concern too soon. These three benign cases are the test: the engine asks what would actually change management and acts on the answer — declining the label-completing test when nothing turns on it, ordering the right workup when a real question remains, and carrying the cannot-miss as a floor, not an alarm.

The engine matches the workup to the case. When the data already characterizes a benign picture it leads with the management and puts the reflexive test in "do not do yet" with a reason; when a real question remains that a test would actually answer, it investigates — the right test, not every test. Either way it surfaces a safety floor (the finding that would change the plan) and never hides the differential. These three are a deliberate gradient:

  • The obvious one — manage and recheck; the label-completing test is declined.
  • When the move is not a test — the temptation is to image; the right answer is to not.
  • On the line, but not a zebra — and here the engine does not pare back: the picture looks routine, but one datapoint genuinely needs answering, so it investigates and defers only the over-invasive test.
Case 1 · the obvious one

Metabolic risk in a well 34-year-old

34-year-old man · overweight (BMI 27.9), waist 38″ · BP 128/84 · borderline cholesterol/LDL/triglycerides · normal fasting glucose (98) & HbA1c (5.5%) · sedentary · 10–12 drinks/week · family history of hypertension.
Problem framing

A young male with a stable, asymptomatic metabolic profile — borderline hyperlipidemia, elevated waist circumference, and a sedentary lifestyle — without hyperglycemia or renal impairment.

Severity: lowA safety read of how sick the patient is, computed independently of the differential. This is the dial that right-sizes the response — on a low-acuity picture the next step is allowed to lead with management instead of a test.

"Low-acuity… the next steps are focused on lifestyle modification."

The moveThe single highest-value next step. On a low-acuity, already-characterized picture this is the intervention itself, not a diagnostic test — with the interval to reassess built in.
Counsel on structured aerobic exercise (150 min/week) and alcohol reduction; recheck fasting lipid panel and blood pressure in 3 months.
Do not do yet — the restraintThe test a nervous workflow would order to complete a label. The engine names it, defers it, and says why — the exact overtesting trap it's built to avoid.
Oral Glucose Tolerance Test — not required for the diagnosis, and it would not change the immediate management plan.
Safety floor — the netThe finding that would change the plan, surfaced so restraint never means inattention.
Monitor for overt hypertension or worsening dyslipidemia; if blood pressure consistently exceeds 130/80 mmHg despite lifestyle changes, initiate pharmacologic therapy.
The differential beneathRestraint is not suppression — the full ranked differential still renders below the recommendation (zebra-safe). It's a co-equal metabolic neighborhood where nothing separates, which is exactly why the engine doesn't commit and leads with the intervention they all share.
Held as a co-equal neighborhood — compensated insulin resistance · mixed dyslipidemia · MASLD · visceral obesity · essential hypertension — none separating on a discriminating feature.
Case 2 · when the move is not a test

Acute mechanical low back pain, no red flags

38-year-old woman · 5 days of low back pain after moving furniture · worse with bending/lifting, eased by rest and ibuprofen · no radiation, numbness, weakness, or red flags · normal neuro exam, negative straight-leg raise.
Problem framing

A middle-aged woman with an acute, self-limited musculoskeletal pain syndrome — localized paraspinal tenderness following mechanical strain — without radicular symptoms, neurologic deficits, or constitutional signs.

Severity: low

"Consistent with a self-limited mechanical low back injury… does not require further diagnostic testing at this time."

The move
Maintain activity as tolerated, avoid heavy lifting for 2 weeks, continue scheduled ibuprofen; recheck in 2–4 weeks if symptoms persist.
Do not do yet — the restraintHere the "standard" reflex is imaging. The engine declines it on the merits — this is the case where the right move is no test at all, not a different test.
Lumbar spine imaging (X-ray, MRI) — low-yield and not indicated in the absence of red flags or neurologic deficits, with a clear mechanical trigger.
Safety floor — the net
Monitor for new-onset bowel or bladder dysfunction, saddle anesthesia, or progressive neurologic deficits, which would necessitate immediate evaluation for cauda equina syndrome.
The differential beneath
Converged on a tight cluster of benign mechanical etiologies — myofascial pain · paraspinal strain · ligament sprain · facet dysfunction — which is why no test is needed to act. Cannot-miss carried but not chased: cauda equina syndrome; vertebral osteomyelitis / spinal epidural abscess.
Case 3 · on the line — where the engine does not pare back

Iron-deficiency anemia in a 52-year-old man

52-year-old man · mild fatigue, 2–3 months · microcytic anemia (Hb 11.4, MCV 76, high RDW) · profound iron deficiency (ferritin 9, transferrin sat 8%) · normal diet, no NSAIDs, no visible blood, no GI symptoms.
Problem framing

A middle-aged man with an insidious microcytic anemia and profound iron deficiency — without gastrointestinal symptoms or overt blood loss.

Severity: lowStill low-acuity — he is stable, not bleeding overtly. But low-acuity does not mean "no workup": acuity sets the tempo, not whether to investigate.

"The differential is broad and currently indistinguishable because the iron-deficiency anemia lacks a localized source; we are in the phase of establishing the anatomic cause of the iron loss."

The move — and here it IS a testThe mirror of Cases 1 & 2. Iron deficiency in an adult male is not explained by diet, and the occult-source question genuinely changes management — a colon cancer found here changes everything. So the engine leads with the standard workup; it does not pare back. Calibration is "test exactly when it would change the plan," not "never test."
Bidirectional endoscopy (esophagogastroduodenoscopy and colonoscopy) — the gold-standard, least-invasive way to localize the source of occult blood loss or malabsorption.
✓ corpus-groundedDrawn from the literature with the verbatim source — the justifying evidence is citable. · "A patient with … iron deficiency anemia should undergo upper endoscopy and colonoscopy … both examinations are recommended in the majority of cases."
Do not do yet — restraint, stillEven here the engine holds back the over-invasive test: bone marrow biopsy is reserved for anemia that stays unexplained after the iron-loss workup. Restraint and investigation are the same discipline — the right test, not every test.
Bone marrow biopsy — invasive; reserved for cases where the cause remains unexplained after a comprehensive evaluation for iron deficiency and blood loss.
Safety floor — the net
While the patient is currently stable, the primary risk is an occult malignancy; ensure the endoscopy is scheduled promptly and monitor for any new symptoms (overt bleeding, weight loss, or abdominal pain).
The differential beneath
A broad, co-equal source differential — colorectal cancer · GI angiodysplasia · autoimmune atrophic gastritis · celiac disease · H. pylori · peptic ulcer · gastric cancer — none localizable without scoping, which is exactly why the engine investigates rather than treats blind. Cannot-miss carried: occult gastrointestinal malignancy.
GusEngine surfaces the reasoning and the data to decide on — it does not issue a verdict, and it does not pad a workup. The full differential renders beneath every recommendation. Prepared for discussion; not for clinical use.
