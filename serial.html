Reasoning over serial visits.

Information reaches a clinician serially, and a machine should track the cadence — not re-state the frame each turn, and not assert data it hasn't been given. This is the same leptospirosis case entered as three visits. In intake mode the engine withholds a committed differential by design; each visit it reports what changed — what's new, what it raised or set aside, what it's converging toward, and what remains unexplained — and carries everything forward. Watch it converge on leptospirosis as the findings arrive.

1 Visit 1 — referral
7-year-old boy. Referred to the Paediatric Intensive Care Unit. Presenting complaints: myalgia and a generalized rash. History of present illness: sudden onset of fever, headache and vomiting, present for 2 days prior to admission. Referring (working) diagnosis: staphylococcal toxic shock syndrome. Treatment in progress: vancomycin. Residence: rural area. Prenatal, natal, postnatal and family histories: unremarkable.

◆ What stands out (worth chasing)
Intraoral bleeding — a consumptive coagulopathy or profound endothelial disruption, not addressed by Gram-positive coverage. — sits against uncomplicated bacterial sepsis
Bilateral conjunctival suffusion — a pathogen with microvascular tropism causing systemic endothelial activation, typical of atypical/zoonotic etiologies. — sits against common pyogenic infection
Treatment in progress: vancomycin — the shock state is non-responsive to vancomycin, pointing to a Gram-negative, intracellular, or toxin-mediated process. — sits against the rapid deterioration (GCS 11, hypotension, bleeding)
